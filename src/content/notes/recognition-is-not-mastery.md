For the past few weeks, I have been following a short daily practice routine.

Each session begins with one compact piece of content. I go through it, identify the main ideas, complete several exercises, and finish by explaining part of it in my own way. AI helps prepare the material, organise the important points, create the exercises, and review what I submit.

The routine does not take very long. What it has revealed, however, is a gap that I had underestimated for years.

Recognising something is relatively easy. Retrieving it without help and using it in a new situation is much harder.

## I understood more than I could produce

At the beginning, I often felt that I had understood the material quite well.

When I saw an idea again, it looked familiar. When several options were placed in front of me, I could usually identify the correct one. I could also follow an explanation and agree with the reasoning.

The difficulty appeared when the support was removed.

If I had to explain the same idea without looking back, organise it in a different way, or connect it to a situation from my own work, my response became much slower. Sometimes I knew what I wanted to say but could not retrieve the structure I had just seen. At other times, I remembered the general meaning but missed the details that made the answer accurate.

This was frustrating because recognition had given me an exaggerated sense of progress.

Seeing the correct answer and thinking “I knew that” is not the same as producing it independently.

## The first exercises asked for too much

My original routine moved too quickly from understanding the material to answering open questions.

A typical session would begin with a few simple checks and then suddenly ask me to explain, compare, reorganise, or apply an idea without much guidance. The questions were reasonable, but the jump between the earlier exercises and the final task was too large.

I would spend a long time staring at an empty input box.

The problem was not always a lack of understanding. The task required several things at once: remembering the relevant content, deciding what mattered, choosing a structure, and producing a clear response.

When all of those steps were combined into one question, it became difficult to tell where I was actually struggling.

I therefore changed the order of the practice.

## A ladder works better than a jump

The current routine moves through several smaller stages.

The first stage checks basic understanding. These questions are narrow and have clear answers. They help me confirm that I have followed the content accurately.

The second stage asks me to retrieve one specific idea with limited support. The task may provide a starting point, a required concept, or a clear boundary for the answer.

The third stage asks me to reorganise the idea in my own way. I cannot simply repeat the source, but I still know exactly what the response needs to contain.

The fourth stage connects the idea to a practical situation. This is where I have to decide whether I can transfer what I have seen into a different context.

Only after these steps do I complete a more independent summary.

The final task is still difficult, but it no longer feels disconnected from everything before it. Each earlier exercise prepares one part of the work.

## AI is most useful before and after the difficult part

AI has helped most with preparation and feedback.

Before each session, it selects suitable material, extracts a small number of useful ideas, and creates exercises at different levels. This removes the time I previously spent deciding what to practise and how to test myself.

After I submit a response, AI checks whether I addressed the actual question, whether I missed an important point, and whether the structure is clear. The feedback focuses on one or two changes instead of rewriting everything.

That limit matters.

A long list of corrections can make an answer look hopeless, even when most of it works. A small number of high-value suggestions gives me something specific to revise and remember.

AI can also produce an excellent reference answer, but I try not to read it too early. Once a polished version appears, it is easy to recognise its quality and mistake that recognition for my own ability.

I learn more when I revise my original response first.

## What has improved so far

The clearest improvement is consistency.

Starting a session now requires very little preparation. The material and exercises are already organised, so I can begin immediately instead of spending time choosing what to do.

I also spend less time completely stuck. Guided questions give me a way into the task, while the later stages still require independent work.

My responses have become more structured. I am more likely to identify the main point first, support it with the relevant details, and then connect it to a practical example.

Progress is still uneven. Broad questions remain difficult, especially when I need to retrieve several ideas and arrange them without support. I still understand more than I can actively use.

The difference is that I can now see the problem more clearly.

It is no longer a vague feeling that I need to “know more.” In many cases, I already know enough to recognise the material. What I need is more practice retrieving it, reorganising it, and applying it after the original support has disappeared.

## The next step is repeated use

My next goal is not to increase the amount of new material.

I want to return to previous ideas after several days and use them again in a different context. If something only works during the session in which it first appeared, it has not become reliable yet.

I also want to measure progress through use rather than exposure.

Completing more material is easy to count, but it does not show whether I can retrieve anything later. A better sign of progress is being able to explain an old idea clearly, recognise where it applies, and use it without being prompted.

This daily routine has not removed the difficult part of practice. It has made that difficulty more visible and more manageable.

Recognition is the beginning. Independent use is the real test.
